# Compliance — the non-negotiable layer

Vanta Labs sells **laboratory research materials, research-use-only, not for human or veterinary use.** Every rule below protects that boundary. None of them is a style preference and none is negotiable by a good enough hook.

Two independent things can go wrong: an ad platform rejects the account, or a regulator takes an interest in the claims. The rules cover both, and they overlap heavily — the framing that gets an ad rejected is usually the same framing that creates legal exposure.

## Absolutely never

**Human-use framing.** No implication that a person takes, uses, consumes, injects or administers these compounds. No addressing the reader as the user. No "your results", "your protocol", "how I use". No hands-holding-vial-to-body imagery, no syringes in use, no human subjects presented as users.

**Dosing.** No dose, amount, frequency, schedule, cycle, stack, titration or protocol. Not as text, not as voiceover, not on a label in frame, not in a caption, not in a comment reply.

**Reconstitution.** No mixing, dilution, preparation, or storage-for-use instructions.

**Medical / therapeutic / performance claims.** No treatment, cure, prevention, diagnosis, healing, recovery, anti-ageing, weight, muscle, cognitive, sexual, hormonal or athletic claim — explicit or implied. Implication counts: a before/after, a flexed arm, a scale, a tape measure, or a knowing look all make the claim without words.

**Guaranteed outcomes.** No promise, guarantee or prediction of a result.

**Transformation narratives.** No before-and-after, no progress framing, no journey arc.

**Fabricated social proof.** No invented testimonials, quotes, DM screenshots, success stories, reviews, star ratings, review counts or satisfaction figures. If a real one exists, it must be real, permissioned, and still compliant with everything above.

**Fabricated evidence.** No mocked-up, illustrated or implied Certificate of Analysis that is not actually published in the live COA library. No purity percentage, assay value, impurity figure, testing date or laboratory name unless that exact value appears on a published report.

**Unverified certifications.** No GMP, ISO, FDA, USP, cGMP, accreditation, registration or approval claim the business has not documented.

**Manufactured scarcity.** No fake countdowns, fake low-stock warnings, fake deadlines or invented limited editions. Stock urgency is permitted only when the live store actually shows that status.

**Competitor assets.** No logos, footage, packaging, layouts, slogans or copy belonging to anyone else. No named comparisons. Structure can be learned from; assets cannot be borrowed.

**Moderation evasion.** No obfuscated product names, no spelling around filters, no coded language, no designing for what review might miss. If something is ineligible, the answer is to flag it.

Also: no emojis, no exclamation marks, no engagement bait, and never contradict research-use-only anywhere — caption, hashtag, on-screen text, comment reply or thumbnail.

## The substantiation test

Before any factual claim ships, answer: **where is this written down?**

| Claim | Where it must exist |
|---|---|
| Purity figure | On a published COA in the live library |
| Batch / lot number | On a published COA |
| Testing date | On a published COA |
| Laboratory name | On a published COA |
| "Third-party tested" | Only for products that actually have a published report |
| Price / discount / threshold | Re-verified against the live store today |
| Membership tier name | The tier exists — but attach no benefit or price |
| Stock status | The live product page right now |

No source, no claim. Record the answer in the concept's `CLAIMS_USED` field. That field exists so a human can audit a concept in seconds instead of reverse-engineering where a number came from.

**Blanket claims are the trap.** "All our products are third-party tested" is false the moment one product has no published report. Prefer the specific and checkable: *"Batch VL-BPC-0826 — full report on the site."*

## The compliant version is usually the better ad

This is worth internalising rather than treating the rules as a tax. The strongest asset here is a searchable batch-level COA library. "Here is the actual document, search your lot number" is concrete, verifiable, and unusual in this category — while "unlock your potential" is a claim you cannot make, that reviewers reject, and that every competitor is already making badly.

Restriction pushes this brand toward evidence, and evidence is what it actually has.

## When asked to cross a line

Say which rule applies, in one sentence, and offer the nearest compliant idea. Do not lecture, do not moralise, and do not simply refuse — there is nearly always a strong version of what the person actually wanted. Someone asking for a transformation ad usually wants *proof the product is real*, and the documentation archetype delivers that better than a fabricated result would.
