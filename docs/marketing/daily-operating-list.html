Vanta Labs · Daily operating list

What to do every day, and the honest reason each one is on the list

Nothing on this page guarantees a sale — anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something. What a daily routine can guarantee is the input that produces sales: qualified strangers arriving at a store that can prove its standards. Right now you have neither the volume nor the proof, so every item below attacks one of those two and nothing else.

The only number that matters this month

50–100 qualified visitors per day.

You have taken 8 orders from 364 lifetime visitors. Even generously, a store converting in the normal 1–2% range needs 50 to 100 visits a day to average one order a day. That is the whole arithmetic. You are not short of tactics — you are short of traffic and short of evidence.

So stop measuring days in sales. Measure them in visits created and proof published. Sales are the lagging indicator; these are the leading ones, and they are the only part you control.

Block B

Manufacture the traffic

70 min · every day, indefinitely

Two posts a day, not one. You are not trying to make a good video — you are trying to find a hook that works, and that is a numbers game nobody wins on one attempt a day. Paid is closed to you until the category ruling comes back, which makes this the entire top of your funnel.

45 min

One is always the documentation archetype — the report on screen, the batch line, the vial, "search your lot number." The second rotates: cinematic macro, packing and cold chain, how an assay works, a catalogue tour. Batch-shoot on Sunday if it helps, but never let the queue empty.

15 min

Same asset, reframed — 9:16 for TikTok and Reels, 4:5 for feed. Every link carries a UTM, without exception. 5,836 of your 5,839 page views have no source attached, which means you currently cannot tell which of these worked.

10 min

Same body, same call to action, different opening two seconds. Vary one thing at a time or you will not be able to attribute the difference — and a result you cannot attribute is the same as no result. Write them down; do not trust tomorrow's version of you to have an idea.

Block C

Talk to actual humans

50 min · the fastest path to order nine

At three customers, this is almost certainly where your next sale comes from. It does not scale, it will feel slow, and it will out-produce every other block on this page for the next sixty days.

30 min

Your /wholesale page has taken 55 views from nine people with no follow-up path behind it. Bulk buyers are the highest-value segment you can reach without any advertising platform's permission. Ten real messages, each mentioning something specific about them — not ten copies of one message.

20 min

Forums, comment threads, wherever this category actually argues about who to trust. Answer with documents, never with a pitch. Reputation in this space forms in rooms you do not own, and it is the one asset a policy change cannot take away from you.

Block D

Catch what you already have

30 min · stop the leaks

Traffic you already paid for in effort is leaking out of the funnel. This block is cheap and it is the part most operators skip because it feels like admin rather than growth.

15 min

Contact form, direct messages, comments. At your volume a slow reply is a lost customer, and there are few enough of them that a same-hour human response is genuinely achievable. Speed is a differentiator you can deploy today for free.

10 min

Nine carts have been abandoned all time and 29 automated emails have gone out with no recovery figure attached to any of them. At this scale, write it by hand. Ask what stopped them — the answer is worth more than the order.

5 min

Five subscribers is your entire owned audience — the only channel nobody can switch off. Offer the batch reports, not a discount: "get each new report as it is published" attracts the buyer who cares about documentation and costs you no margin.

Block E

Record, and then leave it alone

10 min · discipline, not analysis

This block exists to stop you doing damage. "Whatever it takes" energy applied to a daily dashboard is how people kill working campaigns on noise and rewrite pages that were fine.

5 min

Visitors, add-to-carts, orders, revenue, new subscribers. One row a day in one sheet. Filter your own admin traffic out first — /vault and /admin are currently inside the same analytics stream, which is why your add-to-cart number looks impossible.

5 min

Which hook, which message, which objection. In sixty days this log is the most valuable marketing document you own, because it is the only record of what already failed — and that is the thing every fresh burst of enthusiasm forgets and re-tries.

Guardrails

What "whatever it takes" must never mean

non-negotiable

Every item here is a way to get a fast sale and lose the business. They are listed because determination is exactly the mood in which people talk themselves into them.

—

No outcome, transformation or before-and-after claims. No dosing, no reconstitution, no "your results", no addressing the reader as the user. These are laboratory research materials and every piece of creative must survive being read that way.

—

No invented reviews, testimonials, ratings or COAs. You have three real customers — ask them. A fabricated report in this specific category is the one mistake that ends the business rather than costing it a week.

—

No paid spend before the category ruling comes back. Four direct competitors were checked against the live ad library and none is running ads. Spending into that without a written answer risks the account, not just the budget.

—

No discount invented to force a sale. Your margin is what funds the COA library, and the library is the entire differentiator. Discounting to buy order nine costs you the thing that makes orders 50 through 500 possible.

—

No bought traffic, followers or engagement. It corrupts the only signal you have — which hook actually worked — and you are running this whole routine to generate exactly that signal.

—

No working around a rejection. No obfuscated product names, no spelling around filters. If something is ineligible, the answer is a different concept, not a disguised one.

Expectations

What this actually feels like

so you don't quit in week two

Days 1–14

Probably no new orders at all. This is the normal shape of it, not evidence of failure. You are filing reports and posting into an empty room. Judge yourself on items ticked, nothing else.

Days 15–30

The catalogue is finished and documented. Expect the first hook that outperforms and the first inbound from Block C. Traffic starts arriving with a source attached, so you can finally see what works.

Days 31–60

Search begins to contribute, because 38 completed pages have had time to index. Repeat purchases start showing — your three customers have already placed eight orders, which is the strongest signal in your data.

The real failure signal

Not a quiet week. It is thirty days of consistent execution with no movement in visitors. That means the hooks are wrong — and by then you will have a log that tells you which ones.
